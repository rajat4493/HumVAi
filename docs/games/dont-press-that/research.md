# DON'T PRESS THAT: competitive study

Date of research: 2026-10-23. Method: five web searches (one per game), read as summaries. Three of the five games were not played for this study. Everything is sorted into **verified** (a source says it), **single-source or low-quality** (a source says it but it is weak) and **assumption** (our reading, not yet tested on players).

## What each game does well, and where it falls short (our reading)

Strengths and weaknesses below are **assumptions drawn from the sources above plus general knowledge**. They are the hypotheses this game is built on, not findings.

- **Level Devil.** Strength: one screen, one idea, instant retry, so a death is cheap. Weakness: traps are arbitrary the first time and nothing explains them, so after the third unexplained death it feels like being cheated rather than outwitted.
- **Getting Over It.** Strength: a voice that gives the failure meaning; the setback is memorable because someone comments on it. Weakness: the cost of failure is enormous (long lost progress), which is exactly the exhaustion we want to avoid.
- **There Is No Game.** Strength: an antagonist who reacts to specific things you do, which is where the laughs come from. Weakness: it is a point-and-click joke machine; the player's skill hardly matters, so there is no revenge to take.
- **Trap Adventure 2.** Strength: the surprise comes from exploiting what the player already knows. Weakness: surprises are mostly one-shot and unlearnable, so replay is about memorising a list.
- **Geometry Dash.** Strength: perfectly consistent controls and fast repetition, so every death is the player's. Weakness: no personality; the level never speaks.

## Where this game stands

The aim of the five-level slice, and the thing to prove or disprove with players:

1. **Level Devil's speed, with explained cruelty.** One screen, 250 ms back in. But every trap follows a rule that the host announces in his own way, so a second death is "I should have known" and not "that was random".
2. **A host whose commentary is a score, not decoration.** Professor Probably makes predictions that are judged by what happened, and the score is on screen. He is funnier when he is wrong, and he is wrong often, because the levels are built so that the common mistake is the predicted one and the clever play proves him wrong.
3. **Revenge in the mechanics.** Level 4 aims at your habits; level 5 lets you make him blow up his own wall. That is the difference from "Level Devil plus a chatbot": the rival is a rule set the player can read and beat, and it has no language model in it.
4. **Never a long punishment.** Levels are 3 to 5 seconds long when done right; a death costs a quarter of a second.

## What would make us change course

- If players with the host muted enjoy it as much as with him on, the host is not earning his place. See the playtest plan for the comparison and the thresholds.
- If completion is high but "felt unfair" is also high, the learnable-trap promise has failed somewhere: find which level and which death location.
- If players do not retry, the loop is broken regardless of the jokes.
